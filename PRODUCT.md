# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Angular (user-pinned). Standalone components, signals, SCSS. No backend: runs entirely in the browser and deploys as static files.

## Users

One person: the owner, keeping a personal diary of the games they have played, the books they have read and the films, series and animes they have watched. Uses it on desktop and on the phone, often right after finishing (or giving up on) something, when the opinion is fresh. Interface language is Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR).

## Product Purpose

A personal review wall ("Meu Mural"). The user finds a game, book, film, series or anime by name, writes a review with scores, and pins it to a wall that grows into a visual record of everything they have played, read and watched. Success: logging a review takes under a minute, and browsing the wall is a pleasure the user comes back to.

## Positioning

Not a social review site and not a backlog tracker. It is a private wall of pinned index cards, one per title, where the collection itself is the artifact. No accounts, no feeds, no public scores.

## Operating Context

- Reviews are written after a session or after finishing something.
- The wall is browsed to remember, compare, and rank past games, books, films, series and animes.
- Own reviews live only in this browser (localStorage). The user can export all reviews and import them back as a backup or to move browsers. Backups opened for comparison are kept separately in IndexedDB, persist across visits, and are not included in the owner's backup.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Murais:** five walls: Jogos, Livros, Filmes, Séries and Animes. The header reads "Meu mural de *jogos*"; the word is a button that opens a notepad slip listing the five walls (with counts), and choosing one swaps the whole site to that wall on the same page. The choice is remembered. Nothing crosses walls: each has its own cards, Pra depois queue, Ranking, Lado a lado selection, written bonuses and colour rotation. The backup is one file with every wall. Each wall is a profile in `core/kinds.ts`:

  | Mural | Weighs 2x | Weigh 1x | Status (3) | Amount | Difficulty |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Jogos | Diversão | História, Jogabilidade, Visual | Incompleto / Finalizado / Platinado | hours | yes |
  | Livros | Envolvimento | História, Personagens, Escrita | Larguei / Lido / Relido | pages (whole) | yes, "Dificuldade de leitura" |
  | Filmes | Roteiro, Envolvimento | Atuação, Visual | Não terminei / Assistido / Revisto | — | no |
  | Séries | Roteiro, Envolvimento | Personagens, Visual | Parei / Terminei / Revi | — | no |
  | Animes | Roteiro, Envolvimento | Personagens, Animação | Parei / Terminei / Revi | — | no |

  Stored values never change meaning: a card's `kind` is `jogos`, `livros`, `filmes`, `series` or `animes` (cards from before the walls are `jogos`); the three statuses are stored as `incompleto`, `finalizado`, `platinado` in every wall, only the names change; the same score key means the same thing in every wall (História is História in a game and a book). The field that holds the amount is still `hoursPlayed`, and the reviewed item is still `game`, because renaming them would break older backups.
- **Lookup:** typing a name shows an autocomplete dropdown from the wall's catalogue, all called from the browser (CORS). Books: Open Library, no key; the last word gets a `*` (it only matches whole words), and with `lang=pt` plus the `editions.*` fields (which only come back when `key` is also asked for) each work brings its best Portuguese edition, whose title and cover win ("O Hobbit", "Harry Potter e a Pedra Filosofal"); covers from `covers.openlibrary.org/b/id/{id}-L.jpg?default=false`, so a missing cover errors into "sem capa"; the author is shown under the title and kept on the card. Animes: Kitsu, no key; it completes half words and has the Brazilian name of many titles (`titles.pt_br`, then English, then the canonical one); results whose any name starts with what was typed come first; its images have no CORS, so they show but the service worker cannot keep them. If Kitsu fails, AniList (GraphQL, no key, English or romaji titles). Films and series: TMDB when the user pastes a free key in Ajustes (the short API key goes in the URL, the long read token in an `Authorization: Bearer` header; `language=pt-BR` gives Portuguese names and posters from `image.tmdb.org/t/p/w500`; a 401 says to check the key; Ajustes carries TMDB's attribution line). Without a key, English Wikipedia pages with `Infobox film` or `Infobox television`. Games: Wikipedia (`Infobox video game`) or RAWG, chosen in Ajustes; RAWG needs a key. The Wikipedia autocomplete runs two searches together, the one with the last-word wildcard (for a name still being typed) and one with the words as typed, exact first, because the wildcard alone fills the list with lookalikes ("God of War*" brings Warhammer and Warframe). A trailing year in parentheses (RAWG's "God of War (2018)") is not part of the title when matching; it counts as the year. Cover choices search Wikipedia by exact title, keep the page's year (from the title, "God of War (2018 video game)", or the description), fill in as each source answers, and say when a source failed (a refused RAWG key says to check Ajustes). In the editor, on every wall, "Escolher outra capa" opens the same cover choice as the Wishlist (the item's cover and the others of the same title from the sources below, a pasted https image link, or none), and the live card follows it. Wikipedia needs no key (CORS via `origin=*`, `pageimages` with `pilicense=any` for cover thumbnails). RAWG only offers background art without the title, so when a RAWG game is on Steam (found via RAWG's `/games/{id}/stores`), the cover becomes Steam's official vertical library art (`library_600x900.jpg`); otherwise the RAWG art stays. Rejected after testing: Jikan (MyAnimeList, returned 504), iTunes Search (film search returns nothing), IMDb suggestions (no CORS), OMDb (key, English only), TVmaze (whole words and original titles only), Google Books without a key (the shared anonymous quota was exhausted, 429). The user may also type a name that is not in the list and review it without a cover.
- **Review fields:** the wall's four scores 0–10 (see Murais). By default, the final score (Média) is the weighted average, the wall's centre categories with a base weight of 2x (Diversão in games, Envolvimento in books, Roteiro and Envolvimento in films, series and animes), shown with one decimal. "Dar a nota final na mão" lets the owner replace it with a manual final score from 0 to 11, with one decimal; only this override can reach 11, while categories and the automatic calculation remain within 0–10. "Usar a média" restores the calculated score. Each category has a per-review weight: Relevante (2x its base), Normal, Pouco importante (half), or Não tem (left out of the average and needs no score), so a game is not punished for lacking something it never tried to have (a clicker with no story). Every category that counts needs a score. The wall's amount, optional (hours played, pages read; none for films, series and animes). Status (three, named per wall); Veredito, optional (Masterpiece, Recomendo, Legalzinho, Meh, Chato), each with its own icon; Dificuldade, only on the walls that have it (Nenhuma, Fácil, Média, Complicado, Difícil, Infernal; stored values never change meaning: `dificil` is labelled Complicado, `impossivel` Difícil, and the top level is `infernal`, labelled Infernal); completion date ("Concluído em", or "Jogado até" when Incompleto), today by default and editable for retroactive entries, never in the future. The date is three optional boxes in one strip, Dia / Mês / Ano (the month from a list), with "Hoje" and "Não lembro" shortcuts: all three is an exact day (`AAAA-MM-DD`), month and year is `AAAA-MM`, the year alone is `AAAA`, and all blank is "Data não definida" (always last by date, under its own section). A day needs its month and any part needs the year; a handwritten line under the strip reads back what will be saved ("Só o mês: março de 2024.") or says what is missing. The chosen precision is kept in cards, readers, date sections, sorting and backups, never inventing a day or month; free-text review.
- **Bônus:** optional, as many as the user likes, "a favor" or "contra" (things the four categories do not cover, such as "Trilha sonora incrível" or "Muitos bugs"; each wall has its own ready-made catalog). Each "a favor" bonus pulls the Média as one more score of 10 with weight 1 would (the weight of a Normal História); each "contra" as one more 0 with weight 1. Each bonus moves the Média by at most 0.25 either way (one flaw must not turn a 5.5 into a 4.5): each is measured alone against the categories' average, capped, and the shifts add up; the Média stays within 0–10. Bonuses alone never make a Média: at least one category score is needed. A ready-made catalog per wall (22 for games, about 20 for the others) plus any the user writes; written ones come back as options in later reviews of the same wall (derived from the reviews themselves, so an unused one disappears). Shown as stickers on Completa cards, as a "+N −N" tally on Simples cards, in the reader with the Média they would have without them, in Nota a nota, and in the Ranking's Balanço. The wall search matches bonus names. Included in the JSON backup.
- **Wall:** all reviews as cards, Completas (adds hours, the four scores in one row, and a two-line excerpt; release year lives in the reader) or Simples (cover, name, date, Média, status and verdict only). Both show the difficulty skulls (filled ones only, no label) beside the date on the same line; Nenhuma shows nothing, a remembered preference. The wall is split into labelled sections that follow the sort (month, grade band, letter or status). Text search. Filter by verdict (Masterpiece, Recomendo, Legalzinho, Meh, Chato, or no verdict). Sort by completion date, highest score (per category: Média, História, Diversão, Jogabilidade, Visual), alphabetical, and completion status.
- **Pra resenhar depois:** from the editor, a title can be saved with only its name and cover ("Salvar pra depois"), outside the wall. These pending games live on their own page ("Pra depois"), as sheets torn from a notebook, a binder, graph paper, a message pad or a post-it, stuck to the wall (not cartolina), and are not counted, filtered or sorted with reviews. Each sheet has the day it was saved written in its date header, and it yellows with time (from a week, and more after a month), so what has waited longest stands out. A text search filters the queue by name; it can be ordered by newest, oldest or A–Z. Its own add button ("Guardar jogo", a sheet torn from a spiral notebook, in the header where "Nova resenha" is on the other tabs; N opens it too) opens the same dialog as the Wishlist, in a Pra depois mode: only the name and a cover choice, with the live sheet beside it and "Outra folha" to re-draw it; a title already in the queue is not added twice ("Ver na fila"), and one on the wishlist leaves the wishlist when it goes to the queue. Opening one continues the review; pinning it removes it from the queue. Included in the JSON backup.
- **Wishlist:** each wall has its own list of what the user wants to play, read or watch, on its own page ("Wishlist"). Its own add button (a catalogue coupon, "Adicionar jogo", in the header where "Nova resenha" is on the other tabs; N opens it too) opens a dialog with only the name (the wall's autocomplete, or a typed name without cover) and a cover choice: the item's own cover plus others of the same title (games: Wikipedia in English and Portuguese, and with a RAWG key the Steam vertical cover plus RAWG's background art, extra art and screenshots of the same title, the closest year first; films and series with TMDB: the title's other posters, Portuguese first; books: the covers of the work's editions, Portuguese first; animes: Kitsu and AniList; and the Portuguese Wikipedia page of the same name), a pasted https image link, or none. A title already on the list is not added twice ("Ver na lista"); one already reviewed says so and can still be added. Items are magazine clippings glued to the wall as a collage (not cartolina, not notebook pages), each cut its own way (torn by hand, scissors, pinking shears, a perforated insert, sometimes a folded corner) from its own kind of magazine, with the name glued on top as a handwritten cream or kraft strip, a printed headline, a colour band or a ransom note (no year). In the add dialog, "Outro recorte" re-draws the clipping before it is saved. Each wish has a relevance: MUST PLAY (MUST READ, MUST WATCH on the other walls), Comum or LATER, chosen in the add dialog or changed later from the sticker on the clipping (MUST and LATER show one; Comum does not); the list always sorts by it first. A text search filters the list; it can be ordered by newest, oldest or A–Z, and "Sortear" picks one at random ("Que tal jogar Hades?"), with "Começar a resenha" and "Outro". Tapping a clipping starts the review with name and cover filled; pinning it removes it from the list, and "Salvar pra depois" moves it to the Pra depois queue (same id). The editor offers "Tirar da wishlist" (with Desfazer). Included in the JSON backup; removals are remembered like the others.
- **Lado a lado:** on the Mural, a "Marcar" mode lets the user tick any reviews (one selection at a time, kept in this browser, not in the backup) and open them on their own page: only those cards, in one flow, plus a "Nota a nota" sheet comparing every score and fact column by column, with the best of each score row circled. Built for comparing a series (every GTA) or any hand-picked set.
- **Comparar:** opens a colleague's Meu Mural backup (.json or gzip .json.gz, dropped on a kraft envelope or chosen) locally; nothing is imported into the owner's ReviewStore. Pending items and wishlist entries are not evaluations and never enter it. Multiple named colleagues persist separately in IndexedDB (the chosen one is remembered); the name is guessed from the file name ("backup-da-marina.json.gz" → Marina) or asked on the spot. The header shows two "OLÁ, EU SOU" name tags (Você in black, the colleague in blue: the colleague's pen is blue everywhere on this page), other colleagues as small tags to switch, and actions to see the colleague's wall, update the backup (same name, new file), add another colleague or remove one (with Desfazer). The comparison follows the wall open in the header, with shortcuts to other walls that have something to compare. A spiral "caderno de perguntas" answers, for each person: "Vocês combinam?" (affinity from the mean score distance of works in common, on a school ruler, with the biggest disagreement and the highest agreement as notes that jump to that pair), top 3, finish rate, most used bonuses, a dot strip of every final score with the averages, cartolina colours as proportional strips, verdicts counted in tally marks, and total hours or pages. Below, three divider tabs: "Em comum" (each pair: the owner's real card on the left, the colleague's on the right, a note with the difference in the middle and an optional "Nota a nota"), "Dicas de <colega>" (works only the colleague reviewed, each with "Quero jogar/ler/ver" to add it to the owner's wishlist) and "Suas dicas". Matching prioritizes catalogue identity within the same wall, then title with known author/year; ambiguous homonyms are left out of pairs and of both tip lists. The owner's cards open the normal reader; the colleague's open a read-only reader with their name.
- **Mural do colega** (`/comparar/mural`): only the colleague's cards of the open wall, in the same sections as the owner's wall, with search, verdict filter, ordering and density, and nothing of the owner's.
- **Ranking:** a separate page ranks the open wall's reviews by Média or by one of its categories, with totals (count, the three statuses, the amount and the longest, most given verdict, overall average).
- **Pages:** six tabs in the shared header: Mural, Pra depois, Wishlist, Ranking, Comparar and Ajustes (Ajustes is a small tape with only a gear, named for screen readers and in its tooltip). From 1230px the tabs sit in one row between the cartaz and the add button (tighter under 1480px); below that they drop to their own row, and on phones all six share one row.
- Reviews can be edited and deleted. Deletions are remembered (and travel in the backup), so merging an older backup never brings a deleted review or pending item back.
- Must work offline for existing reviews: a service worker (`public/sw.js`, production only) keeps the site and every cover already seen; the site is installable (manifest). Lookup needs a network connection and must fail gracefully without one.
- Data safety: pinning a card asks the browser for persistent storage; a notepad reminder above the wall asks for a backup when the last one is over 30 days old and something changed since (or, with no backup ever, from 5 cards); "Depois" snoozes it for a week.

## Brand Commitments

- The wall is a literal corkboard/mural: reviews look like physical cards, each slightly tilted at a random angle and held to the wall by a pin. (User-pinned, binding.)
- "LINDO, mas sem sacrificar usabilidade": beauty is a requirement, but it never gets in the way of reading, filtering, or editing.

## Evidence on Hand

No existing reviews, logos, or assets. Cover art comes from the lookup source at runtime. Example reviews used during development are synthetic and must not ship as defaults.

## Product Principles

1. The wall is the product. Every review earns its place as a physical-feeling object.
2. Fast to log. Finding the game and scoring it should feel like jotting on a card, not filling in a form.
3. Private and portable. Nothing leaves the browser except lookup queries, and the user can always take their data with them.
4. Delight never blocks the task. Tilts, pins, and textures stay out of the way of reading, searching, and sorting.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-operable autocomplete (combobox pattern), readable contrast on the wall texture, respects `prefers-reduced-motion`, usable at 360px width.
